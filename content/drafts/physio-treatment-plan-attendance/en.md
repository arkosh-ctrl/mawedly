You built a ten-session plan across five weeks. They came twice in week one, once in week two, disappeared for a fortnight, then returned to say: "Honestly, I didn't feel much difference."

Every physiotherapist and every dietitian knows this conversation. It stings twice over: you lost the income, then you lost the result that would have built your reputation, and finally you absorbed the blame from someone who never completed what you agreed.

## Your profession is not a one-appointment profession

In many businesses an appointment is a self-contained unit. The client arrives, the service is delivered, the transaction closes.

In physiotherapy and in nutritional follow-up, a session is a link in a chain, and the chain loses its value when links are missing:

- Therapeutic exercise builds on the previous session. A gap means moving backwards, not standing still.
- Nutritional follow-up depends on measuring change over time. Gaps strip the data of meaning.
- Behavioural change needs closely spaced reinforcement. A review three weeks late does not correct a habit — it restarts one.

**The practical consequence:** a missed session in your field is not only lost revenue. It degrades the product itself. That is why it deserves more attention here than almost anywhere else.

## Price the double cost

Take the last ten clients who started a multi-session plan:

1. How many completed the full plan?
2. How many booked sessions went unattended in total?
3. Multiply missed sessions by your session price — that is the direct loss.
4. Now the harder question: how many of the non-completers left satisfied? How many would have referred you if they had finished?

Point four cannot be expressed in riyals, and it is the heavier one. Treatment professions live on referrals, referrals come from results, and results require consistency.

## Why clients drop out

The reasons are more predictable than they seem, and each needs a different response:

- **Early partial improvement.** The pain eased after three sessions, so they assumed it was resolved. This is the most common cause in physiotherapy, and it is a misunderstanding rather than negligence.
- **Payment split session by session.** Paying each time turns every appointment into a fresh financial decision, and repeated decisions are easy to postpone.
- **Scheduling friction.** Two sessions a week during working hours is a genuine burden. When a convenient slot is hard to find, they defer — and then forget.
- **No visible picture of progress.** Without measured improvement, they start doubting whether continuing is worthwhile.

Three of those four are solvable with a system rather than persuasion.

## A system that improves adherence

### 1. Sell the plan, not individual sessions

When a client buys a ten-session plan in one or two payments, their relationship to it changes completely. They now own a programme they do not want to waste, instead of facing a new payment decision every visit.

Price the package slightly better than the sum of single sessions. A modest difference is enough to guide the choice, and it rewards commitment rather than punishing hesitation.

### 2. Book all ten appointments on day one

This is the simplest step and the most frequently skipped. Do not end the first session until every appointment in the plan is in the calendar.

The reason is purely psychological. A booked appointment exists. An appointment you will "arrange later" does not. A client who leaves holding ten confirmed slots can see the plan in front of them. A client who leaves with nothing renegotiates with themselves every week.

### 3. Explain the difference between pain relief and recovery — early

An explanation offered after someone drops out is never heard. Say it in the first session, in a sentence they will remember: "The pain usually eases before the treatment is finished. If you stop when the pain eases, it commonly returns within weeks."

That one sentence prevents the single most common form of dropout, because it addresses the misunderstanding before it occurs.

### 4. Make progress visible

A simple measurement recorded each session — range of movement, pain out of ten, weight, circumference, whatever fits the case — turns a vague feeling into a line on a chart. Clients who see measured improvement finish. Clients relying on how they feel stop on the first good day.

### 5. A written, fair rescheduling rule

> "Sessions can be rescheduled with 12 hours' notice. A no-show without notice counts as a session from the package."

Present it with the plan before starting, not after the first absence. It is fair, it is written, and it spares you a personal confrontation every time.

### 6. One reminder the day before

A single message 24 hours out solves most forgetting, and gives the client a chance to reschedule early rather than vanish silently — and an early reschedule hands the slot back to your calendar.
